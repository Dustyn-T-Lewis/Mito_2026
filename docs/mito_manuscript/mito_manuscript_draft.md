title: "Mitochondrial transplantation selectively reverses the phenylephrine-induced hypertrophic-stress proteome in H9c2 cardiomyoblasts"
author: "Dustyn T. Lewis"
date: "2026-06-19"

*A DIA-MS proteomic study of a 2×2 phenylephrine-stress × mitochondrial-transplant design (H9c2 cardiomyoblasts, n = 6/group, 24 samples, 4,806 proteins quantified). This proteomic analysis is a supporting arm of a larger study.*

# Abstract

We profiled the proteome of H9c2 cardiomyoblasts under a 2×2 factorial design crossing phenylephrine (PHE) hypertrophic stress with mitochondrial transplantation (4,806 proteins, 24 samples, n = 6/group; data-independent acquisition MS). Whole-proteome group structure was significant (PERMANOVA R² = 0.219, p < 0.001), but the disease axis alone did not move global structure (Ctl vs PHE R² = 0.095, p = 0.39), whereas both transplant-containing contrasts separated (Ctl vs Mito R² = 0.183, p = 0.006; PHE vs PHE+Mito R² = 0.165, p = 0.004). At the protein level (Π-score < 0.05) the rescue contrast (PHE_Mito − PHE) carried the largest signature (79 proteins), exceeding transplant (67), interaction (39), and disease (25). Pathway enrichment was richest for rescue (52 deduplicated programs, 14 mitochondrial) and directionally opposed disease: the mitochondrial-metabolic core (oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, adipogenesis) was driven up while the extracellular-matrix/de-differentiation programs that disease elevated were suppressed. A six-pilot clustering framework (three c-means significance gates, WGCNA, logFC-vector k-means, threshold-free rank-rank hypergeometric overlap) converged on the same biology: a 528-protein logFC cluster labeled "Reversed Up" enriched for OXPHOS, malate-aspartate shuttle and the mitoribosome; four WGCNA modules with opposite-sign Disease/Rescue correlations; and RRHO2 reversal hotspots of 1524 (Disease-up/Rescue-down) and 1160 (Disease-down/Rescue-up) proteins vs <40 concordant in either direction. Mitochondrial transplant therefore selectively counteracts the PHE hypertrophic-stress proteome rather than adding undifferentiated mitochondrial mass.

# Methods

H9c2 cardiomyoblasts were studied under a balanced 2×2 design crossing PHE-induced hypertrophic stress with mitochondrial transplantation (groups Ctl, Mito, PHE, PHE_Mito; six replicates each, 24 samples). PHE is an established α₁-adrenergic driver of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy (Simpson, 1985; PMID 2988814), including in H9c2 cells (Hahn et al., 2014; PMID 24794531), and isolated-mitochondria transplantation rescues cardiac injury in multiple models (Masuzawa et al., 2013; PMID 23355340; Doulamis et al., 2024; PMID 39732955). Five contrasts were analyzed: disease (CTLvPHE, PHE − Ctl), rescue (PHEvPHE_MITO, PHE_Mito − PHE; primary), transplant-in-healthy (CTLvMITO, Mito − Ctl), the orthogonal interaction, and a secondary contrast (MITOvPHE_MITO); the interaction was treated as underpowered at n = 6.

Protein lysates (50 µg per sample) were prepared with the EasyPep Mini MS Sample Prep Kit (Thermo Fisher; #A4006), with reduction and alkylation at 95 °C for 10 min, Trypsin/Lys-C digestion at 37 °C for 2 h, and peptide clean-up per the kit instructions. Peptides were analyzed on an externally calibrated Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 240 coupled to a Dionex UltiMate 3000 RSLC Nano system (Thermo Fisher): samples were loaded onto an Acclaim PepMap 100 trap column (75 µm i.d. × 2 cm) and separated at 300 nL/min on an Easy-Spray PepMap RSLC C18 analytical column (50 µm × 15 cm) over a 135-min linear gradient from 3% to 50% B (mobile phase A, 99.9% H₂O / 0.1% formic acid; B, 80% acetonitrile / 0.1% formic acid), with direct nanospray into the instrument under Thermo Xcalibur control. MS1 spectra were acquired from 380–985 m/z at 60,000 resolution; MS2 spectra were acquired in data-independent mode with a 10 m/z isolation window, 1 m/z overlap, collision energy 28, and 15,000 resolution. Raw .raw files were processed with DIA-NN v2.2.0, analyzing all runs jointly to enable retention-time alignment and match-between-runs; in silico digestion used trypsin-P specificity with one missed cleavage, carbamidomethylation of cysteine as a fixed modification and oxidation of methionine as a variable modification (one per peptide), and N-terminal methionine excision enabled. Protein reannotation used a reviewed *Rattus norvegicus* UniProt reference proteome (==rat proteome release and spectral library to confirm==) supplemented with iRT calibration peptides, reversed decoys, and common contaminants, with peptide- and protein-level false discovery rates controlled at 1%. DIA-NN returned 5,002 protein groups across the 24 samples (four groups — Ctl, Mito, PHE, PHE_Mito — at six biological replicates each, with selected technical re-injections per the study metadata). Keratin and bovine fetal-calf-serum contaminants (the intersection of the Hao et al., 2022 cell-culture contaminant set with the Human Protein Atlas secreted-to-blood panel) were removed; sample outliers were flagged by a consensus of per-sample missingness, PC1–PC3 Mahalanobis distance (χ² tail, df = 3), and inter-sample correlation; and proteins failing the missingness/contaminant criteria were dropped, yielding 4,806 proteins × 24 samples. Abundances were normalized by cyclic-loess in the proteoDA R package (Thurman et al.).

Missingness in the normalized matrix was modest (4.7%) and left-censored; where a complete data matrix was required (PCA, clustering) values were imputed with missForest (ntree = 100, maxiter = 10; Stekhoven & Bühlmann, 2012; PMID 21653522), which tracked the non-imputed effect sizes most closely (Spearman 0.98–0.99) without manufacturing differential calls. Differential abundance was reported from the **non-imputed** matrix — as limma fits each protein independently using only available observations — with limma empirical-Bayes moderation (Ritchie et al., 2015; PMID 25605792), a group-means design, and a duplicateCorrelation replicate block retained at its low consensus correlation (ρ ≈ 0.02; Smyth et al., 2005). Protein-level significance was determined using the Π-score, a composite metric integrating statistical significance and fold-change magnitude (Π = P.Value^|log₂FC| < 0.05; Xiao et al., 2014; PMID 22321699), as the primary threshold; nominal p < 0.05 and a Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate were also computed as complementary thresholds (Figure 2). Protein set enrichment analysis used ranked-list enrichment (fgseaMultilevel) on proteins ordered by the limma moderated-t statistic (Korotkevich et al., 2021) against pooled rat gene sets (Hallmark, Reactome, KEGG, GO-Slim, MitoCarta3.0; Liberzon 2015, PMID 26771021; Gillespie 2022, PMID 34788843; Rath 2021, PMID 33174596; size 10–500, with disease, cancer, and infection-related terms excluded), with Benjamini–Hochberg correction applied per database (pathway FDR < 0.05) and redundancy collapsed within- then cross-database by the EnrichmentMap combined overlap/Jaccard coefficient (Merico et al., 2010; PMID 21085593).

Six complementary clustering pilots interrogated the proteome at the trajectory level (Figure 6). Three fuzzy c-means pilots applied identical e1071::cmeans on gene-level condition means (Kumar & Futschik, 2007; PMID 18084642), differing only by significance gate (nominal p < 0.05, Π < 0.05, or BH-FDR < 0.10 in ≥1 core contrast); the fuzzifier m was set by the Schwämmle–Jensen estimator (PMID 20880957) and the number of clusters c was chosen from the minimum-centroid-distance elbow (drop < 10% of curve range), capped at ⌊√(N/2)⌋ per the standard cluster-count upper bound (Mardia, Kent & Bibby, 1979). A fourth pilot grouped proteins by Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis modules (Langfelder & Horvath, 2008; PMID 19114008) from the pre-built signed-Pearson network and correlated each module eigengene with binary indicator vectors for Disease (PHE − Ctl), Transplant (Mito − Ctl), and Rescue (PHE_Mito − PHE); modules with opposite-sign Disease and Rescue r at |r| ≥ 0.25 were called "Reversal", a sub-significance floor adopted because n = 24 makes the conventional |r| ≥ 0.40 cutoff collapse every module to "Other" (treated throughout as a ranking aid rather than a per-module inferential claim). A fifth pilot clustered each protein's four-dimensional log₂FC vector (CTLvPHE, PHEvPHE_MITO, CTLvMITO, Interaction) by k-means (nstart = 50, iter.max = 100), with k selected by the gap statistic firstSEmax rule (Tibshirani, Walther & Hastie, 2001; doi:10.1111/1467-9868.00293) and each cluster labeled by the (Disease, Rescue) sign quadrant of its centroid (Reversed Up, Reversed Down, Concordant Up, Concordant Down, Neutral). A sixth pilot ran Rank-Rank Hypergeometric Overlap (RRHO2; Cahill et al., 2018; PMID 29942049; method origin Plaisier et al., 2010; PMID 20660011) between the Disease and Rescue contrasts ranked by signed limma moderated-t (Smyth, 2004; doi:10.2202/1544-6115.1027 — chosen for variance stabilization at n = 24 and consistency with the fgsea cache), tagging genes by quadrant (concordant-up UU, concordant-down DD, reversed UD and DU); for sparse quadrants where the RRHO2 peak-overlap set was < 5 genes, a top-20% percentile fallback (intersection of the top 20% of both ranked lists in the quadrant's implied direction) was substituted and flagged in both the workbook and figure subtitle. Per-cluster, per-module, and per-quadrant over-representation was tested with fgsea::fora against Hallmark and MitoCarta3.0 in parallel per the two-source pairing guidance of Reimand et al. (2019; PMID 30664679), with the same per-database BH correction. All stochastic steps used `set.seed(42)`.

# Results

**Proteome ordination separates by transplant, not by disease alone.** Principal-component analysis resolved the four groups across two axes (PC1 23.8%, PC2 12.2%; Figure 1). Group structure was significant (PERMANOVA R² = 0.219, p < 0.001) with homogeneous dispersion (betadisper p = 0.38). The intervention axis dominated the pairwise tests — transplant (Ctl vs Mito R² = 0.183, p = 0.006) and rescue (PHE vs PHE+Mito R² = 0.165, p = 0.004) separated cleanly — whereas the disease axis did not move global structure (Ctl vs PHE R² = 0.095, p = 0.39). PHE therefore remodels specific programs without shifting the bulk proteome, framing the analysis as a search for focal, directionally coherent changes.

**A focal disease signature and a larger, opposing rescue signature.** At Π < 0.05 the rescue contrast carried the largest signature (79 proteins; 51 up, 28 down), exceeding transplant (67; 47/20), interaction (39; 12/27), and the focal disease signature (25; 16/9) (Figure 2). Across 149 unique differentially abundant proteins there was no three-way intersection and only two proteins shared between disease and rescue, both of which reversed direction (Abhd10, Pcdh1) (Figure 3) — so the reversal is read at the directional and pathway level, not single-protein overlap. Enrichment made this explicit (Figures 4–5): after pathway FDR < 0.05 and cross-database deduplication, the rescue contrast carried 52 significant programs (14 mitochondrial) and dominated the count summary, with transplant 14 (5 mito), disease 10, and interaction 7 (Figure 4). Disease combined an up-regulated extracellular-matrix/de-differentiation arm (ECM organization, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, myogenesis) with a down-regulated proliferative arm (MYC and E2F targets, cell cycle). Rescue then drove the mitochondrial-metabolic axis sharply up — oxidative phosphorylation was the strongest program in the study (NES = 2.76, p = 1.7×10⁻¹⁶), alongside fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, glycolysis, and adipogenesis — while suppressing the same matrix/de-differentiation biology disease had elevated. The transplant-in-healthy contrast previewed the metabolic restoration (OXPHOS, mitoribosome, mitochondrial transport up) even without disease.

**Multiple clustering frameworks converge on a reversal-aligned mitochondrial-metabolic cohort.** Six complementary clustering pilots (three c-means significance gates with c selected per pilot via the minimum-centroid-distance elbow, WGCNA on the pre-built signed-Pearson network, k-means on the per-protein four-dimensional log₂FC vector with k from the gap statistic, and threshold-free RRHO2 on signed limma moderated-t) resolved consistent reversal biology (Figure 6). The logFC-vector k-means returned six clusters; cluster 3 (n = 528 proteins, "Reversed Up" quadrant: Disease-down, Rescue-up) carried the strongest mitochondrial signature, with Hallmark OXPHOS (33/161 overlap, BH-padj = 0.013) and Adipogenesis (25/118, padj = 0.022) at the top of the Hallmark list and MitoCarta Malate-Aspartate Shuttle (5/6 overlap, padj = 0.006; Mdh2, Slc25a10/11/12, Got2), Mitoribosome (15/55, padj = 0.021), and SLC25A small-molecule transport (6/14, padj = 0.053) at the top of the compartment list. WGCNA — at a relaxed |r| ≥ 0.25 sign-pattern floor required by n = 24, hedged as a ranking aid — surfaced four "Reversal" modules (blue, brown, black, turquoise) with opposite-sign Disease and Rescue eigengene correlations alongside one "Concordant down" module (red). Threshold-free RRHO2 between the Disease and Rescue rankings localized the reversal asymmetry directly: the off-diagonal hotspots dwarfed the diagonals — 1524 proteins in the Disease-up/Rescue-down quadrant and 1160 in the Disease-down/Rescue-up quadrant (both from RRHO2 peak overlap) versus < 40 proteins in each of the concordant quadrants (drawn from the documented top-20% percentile fallback because the peak-overlap sets were sparse).

# Discussion

The PHE insult produced a focal rather than global perturbation: it did not separate the bulk proteome (Ctl vs PHE R² = 0.095, p = 0.39) and reached only 25 proteins at Π < 0.05, yet its enrichment profile was coherent — matrix deposition and epithelial-mesenchymal/de-differentiation coupled to loss of proliferative drive, the expected fingerprint of α-adrenergic hypertrophic remodeling.

The central finding is that mitochondrial transplantation reverses this signature selectively rather than by adding undifferentiated mitochondrial mass. Rescue was the largest signature in the study and its direction mirrored disease: the mitochondrial-metabolic core (OXPHOS, fatty-acid and amino-acid catabolism, mitochondrial transport, mitophagy) was driven up while the matrix/de-differentiation programs disease had elevated were actively suppressed, a reversal visible even in the two proteins shared between the contrasts. That the transplant-in-healthy contrast up-regulated the same mitochondrial block indicates that transplantation supplies genuine bioenergetic capacity that, in the diseased context, opposes the hypertrophic-stress proteome; the clustering view reinforces this by organizing the proteome around a dominant metabolic trajectory.

These conclusions are bounded by the design: six replicates of a single immortalized line (H9c2) at one timepoint in vitro, an underpowered interaction term, a non-significant whole-proteome disease separation, and a small direct disease∩rescue protein overlap — which is why the reversal claim rests on the signed pathway-level reversal and the directional flip of the shared proteins rather than single-protein set overlap. As a supporting proteomic arm, the data identify oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, mitochondrial transport, and mitophagy as the rescued programs and extracellular-matrix/de-differentiation remodeling as the suppressed one.

# Appendix: Supplementary Tables

Each figure writes a single Excel workbook to its `c_data/` directory. The first sheet of every workbook is an **Overview** documenting each data sheet (name, role in the figure, and contents).

**Table S1 (Figure 1) — F01_supplementary.xlsx.** `pca_scores`: PC1/PC2 scores per sample with Group. `permanova`: adonis2 R² and p for the overall Group term and the Disease/Transplant/Rescue pairwise tests, plus the betadisper dispersion p.

**Table S2 (Figure 2) — F02_supplementary.xlsx.** `dep_counts`: Up/Down counts and % of proteome at each independent cutoff (p < 0.05, FDR < 0.10, Π < 0.05) per contrast. Four per-contrast DE tables (`Transplant_Mito`, `Disease_Phe`, `Rescue_Mito+Phe`, `Interaction_Mito`): every tested protein with uniprot_id, gene, logFC, P.Value, adj.P.Val, pi_score.

**Table S3 (Figure 3) — F03_supplementary.xlsx.** `membership`: per-protein TRUE/FALSE membership in Disease/Transplant/Rescue, the Up/Down direction in each set, and the region key. `region_counts`: protein count per Euler region.

**Table S4 (Figure 4) — F04_supplementary.xlsx.** `dep_pathway_counts`: per contrast × direction, total significant pathways, mito subset, and mito fraction (the values drawn as bar heights and overlay heights in Figure 4). Four per-contrast sheets (`Transplant_Mito_sig_pathways`, `Disease_Phe_sig_pathways`, `Rescue_Mito+Phe_sig_pathways`, `Interaction_Mito_sig_pathways`): the full FDR < 0.05, redundancy-collapsed 5-database pathway pool the counts were tallied from (pathway, database, clean_label, NES, padj, size, direction, is_mito).

**Table S5 (Figure 5) — F05_supplementary.xlsx.** `contrast_map`: key linking file tags, brief names, and contrast algebra. Four per-contrast sheets (`Disease`, `Transplant`, `Rescue`, `Interaction`): all tested pathways with padj, pval, NES, size, and a `shown` flag for the ring terms.

**Table S6 (Figure 6) — F06_supplementary.xlsx.** `Overview`: data-dictionary listing every sheet, its role, and column contents. `Pilot_summary`: per-pilot method, gate, gene count, fuzzifier m (c-means only), chosen c, and selection basis. For each of the six pilots, two ORA sheets — `<pilot_key>_ora` (Hallmark) and `<pilot_key>_ora_mito` (MitoCarta), paired per the two-source guidance in Reimand 2019 — plus a pilot-specific cluster-assignment sheet: `pilot_p/pilot_pi/pilot_fdr_membership` (gene, hard cluster, max membership, core flag), `pilot_wgcna_membership` (gene → module) with `pilot_wgcna_me_traits` (module × Disease/Transplant/Rescue/Interaction Pearson r and sign pattern), `pilot_logfc_membership` (gene → cluster) with `pilot_logfc_centroids` (mean log₂FC per contrast + quadrant label + n), and `pilot_rrho2_genelists` (quadrant, role, gene, source ∈ {rrho2_peak, pct_fallback}).
