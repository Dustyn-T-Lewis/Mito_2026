*A DIA-MS proteomic study of a 2×2 phenylephrine-stress × mitochondrial-transplant design (H9c2 cardiomyoblasts, n = 6/group, 24 samples, 4,806 proteins quantified).*

# Abstract

We profiled the proteome of H9c2 cardiomyoblasts under a clean 2×2 factorial design crossing phenylephrine (PHE) hypertrophic stress with mitochondrial transplantation, quantifying 4,806 proteins across 24 samples (n = 6/group) by data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry. At the whole-proteome level, group structure was significant (PERMANOVA R² = 0.219, p < 0.001), but the disease axis alone did not move global proteome structure (Ctl vs PHE R² = 0.095, p = 0.39), whereas both transplant-containing contrasts separated cleanly (Ctl vs Mito R² = 0.183, p = 0.006; PHE vs PHE+Mito R² = 0.165, p = 0.004). At the protein level (Π-score < 0.05), the rescue contrast (PHE_Mito − PHE) carried the largest signature (79 proteins; 51 up, 28 down), exceeding the transplant (67; 47/20), disease (25; 16/9), and interaction (39; 12/27) contrasts; across 149 unique differentially abundant proteins the three-way intersection was empty and direct Disease∩Rescue protein overlap was only 2, yet both shared proteins reversed direction between disease and rescue. Pathway enrichment (fgsea, FDR < 0.05, Jaccard-deduplicated) was richest for rescue (42 programs) and showed a coherent directional reversal: oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid metabolism, and adipogenesis were up under rescue (OXPHOS NES = 2.76, p = 1.7×10⁻¹⁶) while collagen/extracellular-matrix programs that rose under disease (ECM organization NES = 1.82) were suppressed. Data-driven fuzzy c-means clustering resolved three trajectory programs in the all-protein variants (and four in the Π-restricted variant), with the mitochondrial/metabolic core program (OXPHOS, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, adipogenesis) dominating the largest cluster. Mitochondrial transplant therefore selectively counteracts the PHE hypertrophic-stress proteome rather than adding undifferentiated mitochondrial mass.

# Methods

## Cell model and experimental design

H9c2 cardiomyoblasts were studied under a balanced 2×2 factorial design crossing phenylephrine (PHE)-induced hypertrophic stress with mitochondrial transplantation, yielding four groups (Ctl, Mito, PHE, PHE_Mito) at six biological replicates each for 24 samples. PHE is an established α-adrenergic agonist that drives hypertrophic remodeling in H9c2 cells (Hahn et al., 2014; PMID 24794531; Jeong et al., 2009; PMID 19299911), and isolated-mitochondria transplantation has been shown to rescue cardiac injury in multiple models (Masuzawa et al., 2013; PMID 23355340; Doulamis et al., 2024; PMID 39732955). The design was analyzed through five contrasts: a disease signature (CTLvPHE, PHE − Ctl), a rescue signature (PHEvPHE_MITO, PHE_Mito − PHE; the primary contrast), a transplant-in-healthy effect (CTLvMITO, Mito − Ctl), the orthogonal 2×2 interaction [(PHE_Mito − Mito) − (PHE − Ctl)], and a secondary exploratory contrast (MITOvPHE_MITO). The interaction term was treated as underpowered at n = 6 and interpreted only descriptively.

## Acquisition, filtering, normalization, and imputation

Peptide intensities were acquired by data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry and summarized to the protein level, yielding 5,002 quantified proteins. Proteins were filtered on missingness and contaminant criteria to a retained matrix of 4,806 proteins × 24 samples. Filtered log₂ intensities were normalized by cyclic-loess across arrays (normalizeCyclicLoess) to remove systematic intensity-dependent biases. Missingness was modest (4.7% of cells) and left-censored (Spearman correlation between protein abundance and missingness ≈ −0.54). For the analyses requiring a gap-free matrix — ordination and clustering — missing values were imputed with the random-forest method missForest (Stekhoven & Bühlmann, 2012; PMID 21653522); among the candidate imputers evaluated (imp4p with a mixed MNAR/MCAR model, and an imputeLCMD/MsCoreUtils kNN–QRILC hybrid), missForest tracked the non-imputed effect sizes most closely (Spearman 0.98–0.99) and, unlike the alternatives, did not manufacture differential calls in high-missingness proteins. Differential abundance itself was reported from the non-imputed matrix (below), so imputation never enters the inferential analysis.

## Differential abundance, Π-score, and replicate blocking

Differential abundance was modeled with limma (Ritchie et al., 2015; PMID 25605792) on the normalized matrix, fitting a group-means design and extracting the five contrasts above through empirical-Bayes moderated t-statistics. Technical replicate structure was accommodated with duplicateCorrelation (Smyth et al., 2005), which estimated a low intra-block consensus correlation (ρ ≈ 0.02); rather than dropping the block, this correlation was retained in the fit as a blocked generalized-least-squares term, a conservative choice that leaves the residual degrees of freedom essentially intact while acknowledging the design. Significance was assessed primarily by the Π-score (Xiao et al., 2014; PMID 22321699), which combines evidence and effect size as Π = P.Value^|log₂FC| and was thresholded at Π < 0.05; Benjamini–Hochberg FDR < 0.10 served as a secondary criterion. The reported analysis uses the non-imputed differential-abundance arm; the imputed matrix was reserved for exploratory ordination and clustering.

## Enrichment and over-representation analysis

Pathway-level signal was assessed by gene-set enrichment with fgsea (Korotkevich et al., 2021) run on the limma moderated-t statistic for each contrast, against rat-specific gene sets pooled from the Hallmark collection (Liberzon et al., 2015; PMID 26771021), Reactome (Gillespie et al., 2022; PMID 34788843), KEGG, a Gene Ontology Slim, and MitoCarta3.0 (Rath et al., 2021; PMID 33174596). Enrichment results were filtered at FDR < 0.05 and collapsed across redundant terms by Jaccard-similarity deduplication (threshold 0.5, applied across databases) so that a single representative term stands for each overlapping family. Over-representation analysis used fgsea::fora with per-database Benjamini–Hochberg correction and odds-ratio reporting against the same rat gene sets, with the universe set to all measured genes.

## Soft trajectory clustering

Protein trajectories were clustered with fuzzy c-means following the Mfuzz approach (Kumar & Futschik, 2007; PMID 18084642), implemented directly via e1071::cmeans with per-gene z-standardization. Protein intensities were collapsed to gene level with limma::avereps on the imputed matrix, and three input variants were clustered: group_pi (the four condition means restricted to proteins reaching Π < 0.05 in at least one contrast; 189 genes), group_all (the four condition means over all 4,805 genes), and sample_all (the full 24-sample profile per gene). Each matrix was standardized, the fuzzifier m was set by the Schwämmle–Jensen estimator (Schwämmle & Jensen, 2010; PMID 20880957), and the cluster count c was selected by scanning c = 2–12 and locating the curvature knee of the minimum-centroid-distance (Dmin) curve, defaulting to c = 9 only if no clear elbow appeared. The estimated fuzzifiers were m = 3.04 (group_pi), m = 2.53 (group_all), and m = 1.10 (sample_all); the Dmin curvature knee placed the two all-protein variants at c = 3 and the Π-restricted variant at c = 4. Core cluster members (membership > 0.5) were then passed to the deduplicated over-representation analysis above, after first removing any term already displayed in the contrast-level enrichment figures so that the cluster analysis surfaces only new biology.

# Results

## Proteome ordination separates by transplant, not by disease alone

Principal-component analysis of the imputed 4,806-protein matrix resolved the four groups along two leading axes capturing 23.8% (PC1) and 12.2% (PC2) of total variance (Figure 1). Group structure was significant overall (adonis2 PERMANOVA, R² = 0.219, p < 0.001), and dispersion was homogeneous across groups (betadisper, p = 0.38), confirming that the PERMANOVA result reflects centroid separation rather than unequal spread. The intervention axis dominated the pairwise tests: the transplant contrast separated cleanly (Ctl vs Mito, R² = 0.183, p = 0.006), as did rescue (PHE vs PHE+Mito, R² = 0.165, p = 0.004). The disease axis, by contrast, did not move global proteome structure (Ctl vs PHE, R² = 0.095, p = 0.39). PHE therefore remodels specific proteins and programs without shifting the bulk proteome, a pattern that frames the rest of the analysis as a search for the focal, directionally coherent changes that whole-proteome ordination cannot see.

## A focal disease signature and a larger rescue signature

At the protein level (Π < 0.05), the rescue contrast carried the largest signature, with 79 differentially abundant proteins (51 up, 28 down), exceeding the transplant-in-healthy effect (67; 47 up, 20 down), the interaction term (39; 12 up, 27 down), and the disease signature itself, which was the smallest at 25 proteins (16 up, 9 down) (Figure 2). The thresholds were strongly nested: the disease contrast yielded 360 proteins at nominal p < 0.05 (7.5% of the proteome) but only 10 survived FDR < 0.10, whereas the rescue contrast retained 300 proteins at FDR < 0.10, consistent with the ordination result that the PHE insult is focal while the transplant interventions are broad. Across all contrasts the 149 unique differentially abundant proteins distributed with no three-way intersection (Figure 3): the rescue-only region held 65 proteins, transplant-only 47, and disease-only 15, with modest pairwise sharing (transplant∩rescue 12, disease∩transplant 8, disease∩rescue 2). The small direct disease∩rescue overlap is expected given the focal disease signature, and both shared proteins reversed direction between the two contrasts (Abhd10 down in disease/up in rescue; Pcdh1 up in disease/down in rescue), so the reversal is best read at the directional and pathway level rather than from single-protein membership counts.

## Pathway enrichment reveals a coherent disease-to-rescue reversal

Gene-set enrichment recapitulated the protein-level asymmetry and made the reversal explicit (Figure 4). After FDR < 0.05 filtering and Jaccard deduplication, the rescue contrast was by far the richest, with 42 representative programs, against 12 for transplant, 8 for disease, and 7 for interaction. The disease signature combined an up-regulated matrix-remodeling and de-differentiation arm (ECM organization NES = 1.82, p = 0.016; epithelial-mesenchymal transition NES = 1.75, p = 0.016; myogenesis NES = 1.67, p = 0.022) with a down-regulated proliferative and biosynthetic arm (MYC targets V1 NES = −1.72, p = 0.0022; cell cycle NES = −1.74; E2F targets NES = −1.60; metabolism of RNA NES = −1.57). The rescue contrast then drove the metabolic axis sharply upward — oxidative phosphorylation was the strongest program in the entire study (OXPHOS NES = 2.76, p = 1.7×10⁻¹⁶), followed by fatty-acid metabolism (NES = 2.38, p = 2.6×10⁻⁸), glycolysis/gluconeogenesis (NES = 2.37), amino-acid metabolism (NES = 2.15), and adipogenesis (NES = 2.13) — while suppressing the same matrix/de-differentiation biology that disease had elevated (collagen chain trimerization NES = −2.24; syndecan interactions NES = −1.82; angiogenesis NES = −2.03). The transplant-in-healthy contrast previewed this metabolic restoration even without disease, up-regulating mitochondrial small-molecule transport (NES = 2.12), OXPHOS (NES = 2.08, p = 1.6×10⁻⁶), the mitoribosome (NES = 2.00), and amino-acid metabolism (NES = 1.93). The interaction term, though underpowered, pointed the same way, with OXPHOS (NES = 1.84, p = 3.8×10⁻⁴) and fatty-acid metabolism (NES = 1.62) up.

## Volcano rings localize the reversed programs per contrast

The per-contrast enrichment rings (Figure 5) display these signed programs against the underlying protein volcanoes, with arc counts tracking signature richness: 10 ring terms for rescue, 7 each for disease and transplant, and 5 for interaction. The rescue ring is dominated on its positive side by the mitochondrial/metabolic block (OXPHOS, fatty-acid metabolism, glycolysis, amino-acid metabolism, MitoCarta transport) and on its negative side by the immune/matrix programs (immunoregulatory lymphoid–non-lymphoid interactions NES = −2.28; collagen trimerization NES = −2.24), so that the two halves of the ring read directly as the restored and the suppressed arms of the disease proteome.

## Soft clustering resolves a dominant mitochondrial-metabolic program

Fuzzy c-means clustering resolved three trajectory programs in the all-protein variants and four in the Π-restricted variant (group_pi m = 3.04, c = 4; group_all m = 2.53, c = 3; sample_all m = 1.10, c = 3; all by Dmin curvature knee) (Figure 6). After removing terms already shown in the contrast figures, the over-representation analysis localized the same biology to specific trajectories. In the Π-restricted variant, the mitochondrial/metabolic cluster (65 of 189 genes, 39 core members) enriched for MitoCarta small-molecule transport (odds ratio 56.7, p = 1.7×10⁻³), adipogenesis (OR 9.1, p = 1.8×10⁻³), oxidative phosphorylation (OR 6.6, p = 6.2×10⁻³), and mitophagy (OR 30.5, p = 0.048), tying the restored metabolic program to a coordinated transport and mitochondrial-turnover module that the per-contrast enrichment had not separately surfaced. The all-protein variants reproduced this structure at scale: the dominant cluster (1,931 of 4,805 genes in group_all; 1,782 in sample_all) enriched most strongly for OXPHOS (group_all OR 2.8, p = 7.3×10⁻⁹), fatty-acid metabolism, MitoCarta fatty-acid oxidation, and amino-acid metabolism (sample_all OR 3.6, p = 2.0×10⁻⁷), while a separate cluster captured the proliferative arm (group_all cluster 1: G2M checkpoint OR 3.6, E2F targets OR 3.2, MYC targets V2 OR 6.0) and another the matrix/de-differentiation arm (sample_all: epithelial-mesenchymal transition OR 2.1). GO-Slim protein folding (group_all) rounded out the trajectory map as new biology specific to the clustering view.

# Discussion

The PHE hypertrophic insult produced a focal rather than a global proteomic perturbation. It did not separate the bulk proteome (Ctl vs PHE R² = 0.095, p = 0.39) and reached only 25 proteins at Π < 0.05, yet its enrichment profile was internally coherent: an up-regulated extracellular-matrix and epithelial-mesenchymal/de-differentiation arm coupled to a down-regulated proliferative program (MYC and E2F targets, cell cycle, RNA metabolism). This combination — matrix deposition and loss of proliferative/biosynthetic drive — is the expected fingerprint of α-adrenergic hypertrophic remodeling in cardiomyoblasts and confirms that the model behaved as intended despite the modest protein count.

The central finding is that mitochondrial transplantation reverses this signature selectively rather than by adding undifferentiated mitochondrial mass. The rescue contrast was the largest single signature in the study (79 proteins, 42 deduplicated enriched programs) and its direction was the mirror image of disease: the metabolic programs were driven up (OXPHOS at NES = 2.76 and p = 1.7×10⁻¹⁶, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, glycolysis, adipogenesis) while the matrix and de-differentiation programs that disease had elevated (collagen trimerization, syndecan interactions, angiogenesis) were pushed down. The reversal is also visible at single-protein resolution in the two proteins shared between the disease and rescue signatures, both of which flip direction (Abhd10, Pcdh1). The rescued programs are therefore the mitochondrial-metabolic core — oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid and amino-acid catabolism, mitochondrial small-molecule transport, and a mitophagy/turnover module that the clustering analysis surfaced — whereas the matrix-remodeling arm is not merely left untouched but actively counteracted. That the transplant-in-healthy contrast (Mito − Ctl) up-regulated the same mitochondrial block (OXPHOS, mitoribosome, mitochondrial small-molecule transport) indicates that transplantation supplies a genuine bioenergetic and biosynthetic capacity that, in the diseased context, opposes the hypertrophic-stress proteome.

The fuzzy c-means trajectories reinforce this reading by organizing the proteome into a small number of coherent programs. The two all-protein variants resolved to three clusters and the Π-restricted variant to four, and in each the dominant cluster was the mitochondrial-metabolic program (OXPHOS, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, adipogenesis), with distinct clusters reserved for the proliferative (G2M/E2F/MYC) and matrix/de-differentiation biology. The clustering view added mitophagy, mitochondrial-transport, and protein-folding modules as program-level companions to the restored metabolism, suggesting that the rescue is not bioenergetics in isolation but a linked metabolic–proteostatic response.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. The design used six replicates per group of a single immortalized cardiomyoblast line (H9c2) at a single timepoint in vitro, so the findings index a controlled cellular response rather than in-vivo cardiac remodeling. The interaction term was underpowered at this replicate count and is reported only descriptively. The whole-proteome disease separation was not significant, and the direct protein-level overlap between the disease and rescue signatures was small (two proteins), which is why the reversal claim rests on the directional and pathway-level evidence — the signed NES reversal across matched programs and the directional flip of the shared proteins — rather than on single-protein set overlap. With those caveats, the data support targeted mitochondrial transplantation as a selective counter-program to the phenylephrine hypertrophic-stress proteome, naming oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism, mitochondrial transport, and redox detoxification as the rescued programs and extracellular-matrix/de-differentiation remodeling as the suppressed one.
